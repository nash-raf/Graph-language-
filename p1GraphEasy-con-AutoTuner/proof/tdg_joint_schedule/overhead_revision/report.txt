Phase A overhead revision — 9 October 2026

Outcome

The revised candidate reaches and modestly beats ordinary-first on the primary
complete-run comparison. With the same frozen reference, inputs and kernels,
all 14 level invocations take 5.8% less time on the paired geometric mean.
The previous candidate, rerun in the same randomized pairs, takes 14.1% more
than ordinary-first. The revision takes 17.4% less time than that candidate.
Steady-state time remains about 21% below ordinary-first (9.6% excluding tiny
workloads). These are measurements on one Ryzen 5 5500/WSL2 host.

Ordinary-first remains the integrated default; the revised candidate is opt-in.
The cold matrix is encouraging but does not establish a confident cold-start
win, and single-invocation costs remain higher. The graph-layout autotuner,
loop kernels, compiler task grouping, and DOALL/DOACROSS equations were not
changed in this revision. Graph-containing levels remain Phase B. Each level
is still optimized independently and completes before the next level starts.

What changed

1. Sparse executor calibration
   Previously, candidate search ran six empty levels for every new combination
   of parent count and task count. There are now three shared anchors per
   executor: one inline callback, two inline callbacks, and two callbacks on
   two parents. Each anchor takes two samples; the larger is a conservative
   estimate. Across both executors this is at most 12 empty levels per process,
   with no new calibration for additional level shapes or parent limits.

   If A1 and A2 are the inline anchors and P2 the two-parent anchor, the
   additional overhead estimate for h parents and K callbacks is

     H_inline(K) = A1 + (K-1) max(A2-A1, 0)
     H(h,K) = H_inline(K) + (h/2) max(P2-A2, 0), for h >= 2.

   h=1 uses H_inline. This is an approximation of fixed, queue and parent
   launch costs, fitted from the actual executor on the running system. It
   contains no host names, benchmark IDs or workload-specific coefficients.
   Actual ordinary-first/candidate observations provide a further policy guard.

2. Lazy loop curves and bounded search
   Existing loop equations are queried only when a schedule needs that width.
   Evaluated widths are memoized for the current search. Broad search receives
   a soft wall-time allowance of 5% of estimated serial work, retains the
   2,048-evaluation cap, and checks the clock every 16 evaluations. An ongoing
   legacy loop calibration cannot be interrupted: cold setup can exceed this
   allowance. This is not a hard latency guarantee or a claim of zero setup.

3. Cached-plan revalidation and local reuse
   Structural identity, event order/count, budget, hints, declared sites and
   loop execution regimes still require compatible metadata. Timing/coefficient
   drift first causes the old candidate and its reference to be rescored with
   current costs. A modest change in level completion estimate can refresh
   the cache without a new search. Larger changes start from the old winning
   plan and refine nearby choices rather than restarting all order seeds.

4. Sparse timing with continuous structural validation
   Initial callback timing is collected normally. After eight observations,
   detailed callback timing is sampled every eighth invocation. Loop boundary
   intervals and occurrence identity are still checked every invocation to
   reject overlapping or changed traces. An unsampled structural change
   invalidates costs immediately and forces fresh timing on subsequent useful
   execution. Ordinary durations may therefore adapt with a sampling delay.

5. Passive policy feedback
   Useful ordinary-first executions record the level's actual execution time.
   The second observation replaces the cold first sample; later observations
   update an EWMA. The observed reference caps an optimistic model comparison.
   Two consecutive slower executions of the same candidate plan trigger
   ordinary-first fallback. A different allocation/admission plan receives
   fresh feedback. Every sixteenth observed level provides a useful reference
   verification when a candidate would otherwise run. Work is never duplicated:
   a level runs once under its chosen policy. Feedback is keyed by level/task,
   loop trace/regime, observed work and granted budget, not by benchmark names.

6. Avoid unnecessary per-worker model state
   During development, a temporary reference plan was stored in TLS, growing
   the experimental binary's TLS footprint to 9,248 bytes per worker. It now
   lives on the planner's call stack. Final ELF TLS sizes are 224 bytes for the
   revised candidate, 208 for the previous candidate and 192 for the reference.
   The executor's resource behavior and loop bodies are unchanged.

The objective remains minimum completion time for one level, with one parent
per active callback and a peak loop-team reservation for its lifetime. All
ordinary tasks have width one. Width changes and ordering changes are scored
together; the shared exclusive DOALL pool and ephemeral DOACROSS teams retain
their existing behavior. These changes reduce the cost of solving that model.

Final experiment

Primary: 36 workload/budget combinations, budgets 2/4/8, three independent
processes per policy/case, 3 warm plus 11 measured levels. Five arms: frozen
reference, revised candidate, ordering-only, overload-only and preserved
previous candidate. Cold: the same nine-case subset without isolated loop
calibration, three paired processes for reference/revised/previous. Policy
order and case order are shuffled deterministically. No jobs overlap.
Everything inside the level, including profiling, lookup, planning and passive
verification, is timed. Common external preparation is outside level timing.

Joint/reference paired geometric ratios, with hierarchical paired 95% bootstrap
intervals (below one is faster):

                            Primary                     Cold subset
  Steady median             0.787 [0.658, 0.911]         0.519 [0.297, 0.840]
  First invocation          1.092 [1.040, 1.154]         1.201 [1.071, 1.376]
  All 14 invocations        0.942 [0.895, 0.991]         0.929 [0.847, 1.009]

The cold complete-run interval includes parity. First-invocation overhead is
still material; this implementation does not promise a one-shot improvement.
The previous implementation's paired all-14/reference ratios are 1.141 in the
primary matrix and 1.315 in the cold subset. Revised/previous ratios are 0.826
and 0.707 respectively. The primary steady performance is approximately equal
to the previous candidate (ratio 1.003), while total cost is substantially lower.

Primary per-case median time improves in 29/36 combinations. Its worst median
ratio is 1.095. The cold B=4 overflow-short case regresses 16.3% on the ratio of
policy medians; no stratum has a >10% regression in all three paired processes.
All cases, including these exceptions, remain in the aggregate results. Three
processes do not establish absence of regressions. Tiny cases influence the
geometric means strongly; non-tiny steady ratios are 0.904 primary and 0.881 cold.

Across 108 primary revised-candidate processes:
  117 full searches (1.08/process), versus approximately four/process before;
  1,004 direct cache hits and 193 cheap revalidations;
  1,260 calibration executions (maximum 12/process);
  3,741 schedule evaluations;
  85.8 ms cumulative recorded learning/search time (0.79 ms/process average).
The counter excludes fast cache lookup and some boundary bookkeeping; all-14
timing includes those costs. In the cold subset, recorded learning averages
2.53 ms/process because existing loop calibration can also be encountered.

Developmental results are preserved in overhead_revision_pre_guard and
overhead_revision_with_tls. The shorter search initially produced a repeatable
20.6% steady regression for B=8 unequal sibling loops. This motivated passive
comparison with useful reference execution; the TLS issue then motivated
moving model bookkeeping onto the planner stack. Final cases and thresholds
were not edited to remove failures. Source snapshots and old results remain
available separately from final benchmark.csv/cold.csv.

Interpretation

The setup penalty that previously erased steady-state gains is substantially
reduced. The primary complete-run comparison now clears parity, but cold-start
uncertainty, first-invocation cost and workload-specific exceptions remain.
This supports further evaluation of the candidate; it is not evidence of a
universal performance guarantee or a reason to change graph-layout selection.
