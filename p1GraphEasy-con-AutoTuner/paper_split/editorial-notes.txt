P1 MAIN-PAPER / APPENDIX SPLIT

Editorial premise
The supplied section presents phase- and scope-aware effects, independence
analysis along spatial and temporal axes, and proof-directed execution as
the P1 contribution. The main-paper replacement is organized around that
argument. This is an editorial judgment based on the supplied section,
not a claim about an OOPSLA submission rule or a review of the whole paper.

Use
Replace the supplied section with p1-main.tex. Insert p1-appendix.tex after
the paper's existing appendix command. The appendix file deliberately
does not issue that command, so it can coexist with other appendices.
The files are LaTeX fragments, not standalone documents. Use amsmath,
amssymb, booktabs, and tabularx, plus the existing bibliography entries.
The recognition table uses table* to allow the paper's full text width.
Final pagination must be checked in the actual manuscript template.

Kept in the main paper
- The problem P1 addresses and its central effect abstraction.
- The canonical loop expression and the distinction between structural
  composition and permission to execute concurrently.
- Effect kinds, region meanings, occurrence scope, and conservative fallback.
- One shared independence criterion instead of duplicated spatial and
  temporal definitions.
- Transformation applicability and semantic-preservation obligations.
- A brief ownership example explaining why source ownership is insufficient
  when destination state is also mutated.
- How analysis becomes executable work, the combined round/partition graph,
  and the implementation limits needed to interpret evaluation results.

Moved to the appendix
- Iterator-less loop detection, the provenance domain, full atom fields,
  finite enumeration semantics, and the instruction recognition table.
- Read summaries, ordered access relations, RAW/WAR classification cases,
  WAW treatment, and repeated occurrences of a write site.
- Detailed footprint and partition definitions and a conditional
  commutation argument.
- Callback signatures, frames, descriptors, task readiness, joins,
  source-coverage passes, initialization, finalization, and resource lifetime.
- GPU lowering, streams, device buffers, and shared upload/download barriers.

Condensed or corrected rather than merely relocated
- Removed repeated explanations of atoms versus tasks, joins, and fallback.
- Folded the two essentially identical footprint definitions and
  commutativity statements into one parameterized account.
- Removed the invalid equation that equated a union of effects with final
  program state. Effects describe accesses; their union is not a state.
- Replaced the original case-specific discharge theorem with an explicit
  proof obligation. As supplied, that theorem restates the independence
  criterion and contains no actual transformation cases or proofs.
- Replaced the unconditional non-interference corollary with a conditional
  preservation argument. Independence of transformed effects and
  equivalence to the original program are separate obligations.
- Kept the requirement to preserve constraints on the other axis; spatial
  OR temporal independence must not imply arbitrary whole-loop reordering.
- Distinguished a cyclic round-summary interference graph from an execution
  DAG. A self-loop is not a DAG edge.
- Parenthesized the temporal conflict condition through a shared predicate
  so sequential order applies to RAW, WAR, and WAW alike.
- Fixed the unmatched end-definition, the Seq expression, hierarchy of
  subsections, and inconsistent operator notation in the recognition table.

Items the supplied section does not establish
1. Actual transformation rules and their applicability conditions are not
   supplied. If these are the paper's principal novelty, their essential
   rules must remain in the main paper, with a worked example; only their
   full proofs should move to the appendix. The new text does not pretend
   that the generic independence equation supplies these rules.
2. The four values and ranking of the temporal read summary are unspecified.
   The appendix retains the description without inventing this enumeration
   or conflating it with the finer footprint classifications.
3. The frontier-based PreviousRoundRead classification needs a semantic
   justification elsewhere; frontier traversal alone does not prove a read
   obtains an earlier-round value. The draft treats this as a classification,
   not as permission to snapshot.
4. Recognition of subtraction or floating-point addition does not establish
   associative regrouping. Applicable reduction conditions must account for
   the actual numeric semantics and observable result requirements.
5. A conditional store recognized as a claim does not by itself establish
   that parallel first-wins behavior matches serial winner selection.
   Any such transformation needs its own applicability argument.
6. The conditional proof sketch assumes complete modeling of observable
   effects, control requirements, and runtime resources. It is not a machine-
   checked proof or an implementation audit. No source-code claims were
   independently verified during this editorial task.

Cross-references
The original eq:effect-expr and tab:effect-recognition labels are retained.
The original def:effect-atom, ni, and corol labels are removed because the
definition/theorem presentation was consolidated or changed. Update any
references elsewhere in the manuscript to the relevant new section or
eq:p1-independence; do not leave references calling an equation a theorem.
All four bibliography keys from the supplied section are retained.

Submission emphasis
The main paper should let a reviewer understand what is new, why it is
sound under stated assumptions, and how the evaluation exercises it.
Runtime mechanics can live in the appendix. Essential transformation
conditions and limitations that affect the claims should remain visible
in the main paper. If space remains, use it for one representative graph
loop traced through effects, conflict discharge, and the resulting plan,
rather than expanding callback and scheduler descriptions again.
